# Actor boundary security and delivery contracts — draft 2026-10-18

Tracking: #69 (untrusted supervisor quota), #79 (IPC delivery receipts), #80 (FFI cancellation and quiescence).

## IPC actor: never collapse three acknowledgments (#79)
1. `LOCAL_ADMITTED`: bounded local outbound queue owns payload; **not** remote receipt.
2. `REMOTE_RECEIVED`: transport peer verified bytes/schema and reserved admission credit; **not** guest turn completion.
3. `TURN_COMPLETED`: authenticated response with actor incarnation, logical request ID, sequence and success/error terminal status.
4. Disconnect after transmit and before `REMOTE_RECEIVED` is `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`, not a definitive failure. Replay defaults to **disabled** unless idempotency key plus server-side deduplication ledger and retention policy are negotiated.
5. No receipt from a stale incarnation may satisfy a future incarnation; bound all receipts to session and actor generation.

Adversarial vectors: (a) lost receipt after remote execution; (b) duplicate authenticated receipt; (c) identity mismatch after restart; (d) out-of-order terminal frame; (e) full inbound quota before decode; (f) cancel racing completion. Only (e) may be rejected before application work, and none may silently imply exactly-once execution.

## FFI actor: cancellation is not native-call preemption (#80)
- For a submitted native call, a timeout produces caller-visible cancellation intent but **must not** free borrowed native arguments until the native function exits and the completion fence runs.
- Actor shutdown and hot upgrade must wait for quiescence or isolate/terminate a whole controlled process; never asynchronously unwind arbitrary in-process native code.
- Any native callback into a guest is denied unless reentrancy, owner, generation and permitted thread are proven.
- Negative vectors: late native completion after timeout, borrowed buffer reused early, callback after teardown, cancel-before-dispatch, cancel/complete race.

## Supervisor outbound quota: mandatory trusted admission (#69)
- Admission lives in supervisor-owned runtime memory, not child actor state, and covers send/respond/output/CONTROL-independent paths and fanout after restart.
- Reserve before encode/copy/queue; failures must refund exactly once and preserve CONTROL liveness.
- Reject spoofed sender ID, quota reset after restart, oversized object graph, and untrusted child mutating a shared counter.
- A guest `state { ... }` remains the sole persistent guest-owned actor state schema; reader/write access through sealed `handle_state` or `using` locks, not quota-authority access.
